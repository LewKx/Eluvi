### Post 2 – Energy ratings in 60 seconds
**Slides**
1. What does a home's energy rating actually tell you? 🤔
2. Most countries rate homes on a scale, often A (best) to G (worst).
3. It shows how much energy the building needs for heating, cooling and hot water – usually in kWh per m² per year.
4. Lower number = less energy = lower bills.
5. Two homes with the same rent can have very different energy costs.
6. Tip: always ask for the energy rating before you sign anything.
7. Save 🔖 this for your next home search

**Caption**
It's in almost every listing – but few people know what it means. Now you do 😉 Do you know your home's rating? 👇

### Post 4 – Embodied carbon
**Slides**
1. Why renovating is often greener than building new ♻️
2. “Embodied carbon” = the emissions from producing, transporting and building with materials.
3. When a building is demolished, that investment is lost.
4. A new build needs concrete, steel and bricks again – all carbon-intensive.
5. Renovation keeps the existing structure and saves those emissions.
6. The greenest building is often the one that already exists. 🏚️➡️🏡

**Caption**
Sounds surprising, but it's one of the biggest topics in sustainable construction. Did you know this? 🤔

### Post 5 – Timber buildings: 3 myths
**Slides**
1. Timber buildings – 3 myths debunked 🌲
2. Myth 1: “Wood burns down instantly.” → Solid timber forms a char layer that protects the core. Timber buildings must meet the same fire codes.
3. Myth 2: “Wood doesn't last.” → Well designed and protected from moisture, timber buildings can last centuries.
4. Myth 3: “Timber is only for small houses.” → There are timber high-rises – like the HoHo in Vienna, Austria.
5. Bonus: wood stores CO₂ as long as it's part of the building 🌿
6. Follow for more building myths

**Caption**
In the Alps, building with wood is tradition 🏔️🌲 Would you live in a timber house? Yes/No 👇

### Post 6 – Passive House explained
**Slides**
1. Passive House: a home that barely needs heating? 🏡
2. The Passive House standard was developed in Germany in the early 1990s and is popular in Austria.
3. Secret 1: excellent insulation
4. Secret 2: airtight building envelope
5. Secret 3: triple-glazed windows
6. Secret 4: ventilation with heat recovery – fresh air without losing warmth
7. Result: very low heating demand and a comfortable indoor climate all year
8. Would you live in one? 👇

**Caption**
One of the coolest ideas from Central Europe 🇦🇹🇩🇪 Save 🔖 if you're dreaming of building one day.

### Post 7 – Lower your heating bill
**Slides**
1. 7 easy ways to cut your heating bill 🔥
2. Lower the temperature by 1 °C – often quoted as around 6 % savings
3. Short, wide-open airing instead of tilted windows
4. Keep radiators free – no furniture or curtains in front
5. Bleed your radiators
6. Close blinds & curtains at night
7. Seal drafty doors and windows
8. Smart thermostats
9. Which tip was new to you?

**Caption**
Small changes, real difference – for your wallet and the planet 🌍 Save for winter 🔖

### Post 8 – Heat pumps: who are they for?
**Slides**
1. Heat pump – is it worth it for me? 🤔
2. How it works: it moves heat from the air, ground or water into your home.
3. Ideal: well-insulated home + underfloor heating (low flow temperature)
4. Possible but plan ahead: older homes with radiators – often improve insulation/windows first
5. Combine with solar panels = extra cheap & green
6. Many countries offer subsidies – check what's available where you live
7. Questions? Drop them below 👇

**Caption**
One of the most asked questions when renovating. Do you have one or are you thinking about it? 🌿
